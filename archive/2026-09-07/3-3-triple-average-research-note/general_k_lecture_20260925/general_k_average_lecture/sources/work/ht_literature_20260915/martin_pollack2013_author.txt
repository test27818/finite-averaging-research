              3. The least character non-residue averaged over the modulus q
Having established an asymptotic formula for the average of the least character non-residue
nχ over all Dirichlet characters χ to a single modulus, we turn now to averaging nχ over
all characters with conductor less than x. Theorem 1.1 tells us that almost all of the φ(q)
characters modulo q have nχ = (q); therefore, we should expect the average value of nχ over
all characters of conductor less than x to be closely related to the sum q⩽x φ(q)(q). This is
indeed the case, as the proof of Corollary 1.2 later in this section will show; the majority of
the work is in showing how the constant Δ deﬁned in equation (1.3) arises in connection with
that sum (see Proposition 3.5). At the end of this section, we outline how the same methods
can be applied to averages over only certain characters; as a concrete example, we establish
analogs of Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.2 where we average only over primitive characters.

  Remark. We say a few words about the rate of convergence to the limit in Theorem 1.3.
Computations suggest that the relative error one makes in approximating Δnsc by the average
over those cubic ﬁelds with |DK | ⩽ x is O(x−1/6 ). Moreover, it appears that these ﬁnite
averages can be better approximated by incorporating the secondary terms of Taniguchi and
Thorne [33, Theorem 1.3] into our argument for Theorem 1.3. However, this reasoning is only
heuristic; our existing error terms swamp any contribution from such lower-order terms.
