                                   Abstract
We here take up some inverse problems related to a weighted generalization of the
Davenport constant for some particular weights for a finite cyclic group.




1. Introduction

Let G be a finite abelian group (written additively) of exponent m and A ⊂ [1, m−1].
By a sequence over G, we mean a finite sequence of terms from G which is unordered

D(G), one may look into [10] and [11], for instance.
   The weighted version DA (G) of the Davenport constant (and similar generaliza-
tions of some other zero-sum constants) were initiated in some papers of Adhikari,
Chen, Friedlander, Konyagin and Pappalardi [3], Adhikari and Rath [5], Thangadu-

and Schmid [17], etc.) on questions, conjectures and applications related to these
generalizations.

  Adhikari, David and Urroz [4] showed that if n = pr for a prime p > 3 or n is
square-free and coprime to 6, then

                                  DRn (Zn ) = 2Ω(n) + 1,                                    (5)

where for any positive integer n, Ω(n) denotes the number of prime factors of n,
multiplicity included.
   Later, Chintamani and Moriya [7] proved that the equality (5) holds when n is
any integer coprime to 30 = 2 × 3 × 5. They also proved that (5) holds when n is a
power of 3.
   Regarding further results in this direction, Grynkiewicz and Hennecart [13] proved
that if A is the set of squares in the group of units in the cyclic group Zn , then for
a prime p, writing vp (n) to denote the p-valuation of n, one has

                       DA (Zn ) = 2Ω(n) + min{v5 (n), v3 (n)} + 1,                          (6)

for odd n with either v3 (n) = 0 or v3 (n) ≥ v5 (n).
   To state our next results, we shall need the following definition.
Definition 1. If the weight-set A is a subgroup of Z∗n , two sequences x1 · . . . · xk
and y1 · . . . · yk over Zn are said to be equivalent with respect to A if there are
a1 , . . . , ak ∈ A, c ∈ Z∗n and a permutation σ ∈ Sk such that yi = cai xσ(i) , for
i = 1, 2, . . . , k.
   It is clear from Definition 1 that if x1 · . . . · xk and y1 · . . . · yk are equivalent with
respect to A, then x1 ·. . .·xk does not have any non-empty A-weighted zero-sum sub-
sequence if and only if y1 · . . . ·yk does not have any non-empty A-weighted zero-sum
subsequence. Thus, when the weight-set A ⊂ Z∗n is a group under multiplication,
we want to characterize the extremal sequences up to this equivalence.
   In the following theorems, we characterize the extremal sequences for the result
(5) of Adhikari, David and Urroz [4] and a generalization due to Chintamani and
Moriya [7] mentioned in the introduction.

We shall need the following result ([6], [9], one may also see [19], for instance).

Lemma 1. (Cauchy–Davenport Inequality). For a prime p, let A and B be two
non-empty subsets of Zp . Then

                             |A + B| ≥ min{p, |A| + |B| − 1},

                      A + B = {x ∈ Zp | x = a + b, a ∈ A, b ∈ B}

  Thus we have the following.

Lemma 2. Let p ≥ 7 be any prime. Then given any y1 , y2 , y3 ∈ Z∗p , we have

                              Rp y1 + Rp y2 + Rp y3 = Zp .

  We observe that from the above lemma, one can easily deduce the following.

Lemma 3. Let p ≥ 7 be any prime. If a sequence y1 · y2 · . . . · yl over Zp has at
least three non-zero elements, then there exist α1 , α2 , . . . , αl ∈ Rp such that

                             α1 y1 + α2 y2 + · · · + αl yl = 0.

Proof. Without loss of generality, let y1 , y2 and y3 be three terms which are not
congruent to 0 modulo p. Then by Lemma 2, we have

                              Rp y1 + Rp y2 + Rp y3 = Zp .

Therefore, there exist α1 , α2 , α3 ∈ Rp such that

                      α1 y1 + α2 y2 + α3 y3 = −y4 − y5 − · · · − yl .

Now choosing αi = 1 for i = 4, 5, . . . , l, we obtain our desired result.

  We shall also need the following result of Chowla [8] (one may also see [16], [19])
which provides a generalization of the Cauchy-Davenport inequality to composite
numbers.

Lemma 4. (Chowla). Let n be a natural number and A and B be nonempty subsets
of Zn . If 0 ∈ B and gcd(b, n) = 1 for all b ∈ B \ {0}, then

                           |A + B| ≥ min{n, |A| + |B| − 1}.

Proof of Theorem 3. Arguing as in Theorem 2, here we observe that a sequence
of the form as given in the statement of the theorem cannot have any non-empty
Rn -weighted zero-sum subsequence.
   We proceed to prove the converse by induction on k. If k = 1 so that n = p1 ,
a prime, then a sequence of length 2 with no Rn -weighted zero-sum subsequence is
clearly of the form αβ where α = v1 is a quadratic residue modulo p1 and β = −v2
where v2 is a quadratic non-residue modulo p1 .
   Now, let k ≥ 2 and let S : x1 · . . . · x2k be a sequence over Zn such that it does
not have any non-empty Rn -weighted zero-sum subsequence.
   If for each prime q ∈ {p1 , p2 , . . . , pk }, there are three xi ’s, co-prime to q, then
by Lemma 3 and the Chinese Remainder Theorem, S is an Rn -weighted zero-sum
sequence.
   Therefore, there exists at least one prime q1 ∈ {p1 , p2 , . . . , pk }, such that at most
two xi ’s are co-prime to q1 .

   First, we assume that the number of xi ’s co-prime to q1 is not more than one.
Without loss of generality, assume that q1 divides xi for i = 2, . . . , 2k. Let xi = q1 yi
for i = 2, . . . , 2k.
   Writing n1 = qn1 , since the length of the sequence y2 · . . . · y2k is 2k − 1 =
2Ω(n1 ) + 1 = DRn1 (Zn1 ), there is a non-empty Rn1 -weighted zero-sum subsequence
modulo n1 of y2 ·. . .·y2k . Therefore, by the Chinese Remainder Theorem, y2 ·. . .·y2k
has a non-empty Rn -weighted zero-sum subsequence modulo n1 and hence x2 ·. . .·x2k
has a non-empty Rn -weighted zero-sum subsequence modulo n.
   Thus there are exactly two xi ’s which are co-prime to q1 . Without loss of general-
ity, we assume that x1 and x2 are co-prime to q1 . So, q1 divides xi for i = 3, . . . , 2k.
Let xi = q1 yi for i = 3, . . . , 2k.
   Then it cannot happen that both x1 and −x2 are squares or both of them are
non-squares in Z∗q1 . Otherwise, there exist α1 , α2 ∈ Rq1 such that α1 x1 + α2 x2 ≡ 0
(mod q1 ). Therefore, by the Chinese Remainder Theorem, there exist β1 , β2 ∈ Rn
such that β1 x1 + β2 x2 ≡ 0 (mod q1 ); let β1 x1 + β2 x2 = q1 z. Now z · y3 · . . . · y2k is
of length 2k − 1 = 2Ω(n1 ) + 1 and will have a non-empty Rn1 -weighted zero-sum
subsequence modulo n1 and by the previous argument (β1 x1 + β2 x2 ) · x3 · . . . · x2k
and hence S will have a non-empty Rn -weighted zero-sum subsequence modulo n.
   Now, using the induction hypothesis on y3 · . . . · y2k so that it does not have a
non-empty Rn1 -weighted zero-sum subsequence modulo n1 , we are through.
